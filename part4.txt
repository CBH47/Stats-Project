Part 4 — P-value and Hypothesis Testing


Objective: Compare the mean valence scores between two randomly selected groups to determine
whether there is a statistically significant difference in their population means.


Data

  Two random samples were drawn from the population of Spotify song valence scores without
  replacement:

    Group 1 (n = 30):  mean = 0.5185, sd = 0.2299
    Group 2 (n = 35):  mean = 0.5071, sd = 0.2274


Hypotheses

  Let mu_1 and mu_2 denote the true population mean valence for Group 1 and Group 2,
  respectively. Since both groups were drawn from the same underlying population of Spotify
  songs, we test:

    H_0:   mu_1 - mu_2 = 0        (the two groups have equal population means)
    H_a:   mu_1 - mu_2 != 0       (the two groups have different population means)


Two-Sample t-Test

  We use Welch's two-sample t-test, which does not assume equal variances between the
  two groups.

  Test statistic:

    t = (X-bar_1 - X-bar_2) / sqrt(s_1^2/n_1 + s_2^2/n_2)

  Computation:

    t = (0.5185 - 0.5071) / sqrt(0.2299^2/30 + 0.2274^2/35)
      = 0.0114 / 0.0569
      = 0.2015

  Degrees of freedom (Welch-Satterthwaite approximation): df = 61.28

  p-value: 0.841

  Decision: At the alpha = 0.05 significance level, we fail to reject H_0 since
  p = 0.841 > 0.05. The 95% confidence interval for the difference in means is
  (-0.1023, 0.1252), which includes zero. There is no statistically significant
  evidence of a difference between the two groups' mean valence scores. This result
  is expected since both samples were drawn from the same population.


Non-Parametric Test (Wilcoxon Rank-Sum / Mann-Whitney U)

  The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used as a non-parametric alternative to the t-test.
  It does not assume normality of either group's distribution.

  Test statistic: W = 533.5
  p-value: 0.9143

  Decision: At alpha = 0.05, we fail to reject H_0 (p = 0.9143 > 0.05). The conclusion
  is consistent with the t-test — there is no significant difference between groups.

  Why this test is still appropriate: The Wilcoxon rank-sum test works by ranking all
  observations from both groups together (from smallest to largest valence), then summing
  the ranks within each group. Under H_0, the two groups should have similar rank sums.
  If one group tends to have higher values, its rank sum will be larger. The test evaluates
  whether the observed difference in rank sums is larger than expected by chance. It does
  not require any assumption about the shape of the underlying distribution, making it
  robust even when normality is violated.


Why a t-test with n = 20 May Not Be Appropriate

  The t-test relies on the assumption that the sampling distribution of the mean is
  approximately normal. For small samples (n < 30), this approximation depends heavily
  on the underlying population being roughly normal. Our valence data has a tent-shaped
  (platykurtic) distribution, not a bell-shaped one. With only n = 20 per group, the
  Central Limit Theorem may not have taken full effect, and the t-test p-value could be
  unreliable. Larger samples (n >= 30) provide more robustness against deviations from
  normality, which is why we used groups of size 30 and 35 in this analysis.
